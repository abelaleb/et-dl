# Ethiopian Driver's Training Manual: Core Curriculum Excerpts

## Chapter 1: Right-of-Way Regulations

### Section 1.1 Roundabout Right-of-Way Priority
According to Ethiopian Federal Transport Authority regulations:
- Any vehicle entering a roundabout must yield right-of-way to vehicles already inside and circulating within the roundabout.
- Drivers approaching a roundabout must slow down and give priority to traffic circulating from their left.
- Never make abrupt or unsignaled lane changes inside a roundabout.
- Signal your intention to exit using the proper turn indicator before turning off the roundabout.

### Section 1.2 The Priority to the Right Rule (Unregulated Intersections)
At any intersection where there is no traffic light, traffic officer, or regulatory sign:
- Vehicles coming from the right-hand side always have priority of passage.
- A vehicle turning left must yield to oncoming vehicles traveling straight ahead or turning right.
- Traffic on a designated primary arterial road has priority over secondary or feeder access roads.

### Section 1.3 Priority for Emergency Vehicles
Emergency vehicles (ambulances, fire engines, police cars) sounding sirens or displaying flashing emergency beacons have absolute priority over all other road users.
- Drivers must immediately pull as close as practical to the right-hand edge of the roadway and stop until the emergency vehicle has cleared.

## Chapter 2: Speed Limits & Safe Distance Rules

### Section 2.1 Speed Ceilings for Urban and Rural Corridors
Unless otherwise posted by regulatory signs:
- **Urban Areas (Addis Ababa & Regional Cities):** Default speed limit is 30 km/h to 50 km/h. Near schools, hospitals, pedestrian zones, and open markets, the ceiling is 30 km/h.
- **Open Rural Highways:** For private passenger automobiles, maximum speed limit is 80 km/h to 100 km/h.
- **Commercial Freight & Heavy Public Transport:** Maximum highway speed ceiling is 70 km/h to 80 km/h.

### Section 2.2 The Two-Second Safe Following Distance
A driver following another vehicle must maintain a sufficient safety gap to stop safely in case of sudden deceleration:
- On dry asphalt in clear daylight, maintain at least a **2-second interval**.
- During rain, fog, gravel roads, or nighttime driving, expand this buffer to at least **4 seconds**.

## Chapter 3: Mechanical & Pre-Trip Safety Inspections

### Section 3.1 Mandatory Daily Pre-Trip Inspections
Before starting any journey, the driver must verify:
1. Critical fluid levels: engine oil, hydraulic brake fluid, and engine coolant/radiator level.
2. Tire conditions: pneumatic pressure and tread depth (minimum 1.6 mm).
3. Electrical illumination: headlights, brake lights, hazards, and turn indicators.
4. Steering and braking responsiveness.
5. Mandatory emergency equipment: functional spare wheel, jack, wheel brace, and two reflective breakdown triangles (C-caution).

## Chapter 4: Demerit Points, Violations & Penalties

### Section 4.1 Driving Under the Influence (DUI)
Driving under the influence of alcohol, narcotics, or intoxicating substances is strictly prohibited under Ethiopian criminal transport law. Penalties include steep financial fines, points demerit, driver license suspension/revocation, and imprisonment.

### Section 4.2 Handheld Mobile Phone Use While Operating a Vehicle
Holding or operating a mobile telephone while driving is illegal and subject to immediate administrative fines and penalty points.

---

## Chapter 5: First Aid & Collision Response

### Section 5.1 Post-Accident Emergency Procedures
In the event of a traffic collision:
1. Secure the scene immediately by positioning red reflective triangles at least 50 meters behind the damaged vehicle.
2. Contact emergency medical and police services (Hotlines 907 / 911).
3. Administer immediate bleeding control using direct pressure with sterile dressings.
4. Do not move injured persons suspected of spinal or cervical trauma unless immediate fire or submersion hazard exists.
